Course Overview

Capital allocation, learned by doing it.

"They now must make capital allocation decisions, a critical job that they may have never tackled and that is not easily mastered.": Warren Buffett, 1987 letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders

FIN 5104 develops the analytical judgment and quantitative tools required of a general manager making corporate financial decisions, organized around the three questions every finance executive must answer: how should the firm invest its capital, how should it finance those investments, and how should it return cash to its owners?

Each week's lecture builds theory from a case in the textbook: then you work through a different case, facing the actual decision a real company faced. In parallel, the Live Company Valuation Project runs the full semester: by the final week you've built, milestone by milestone, a professional-grade valuation and capital-policy analysis of a public company you chose yourself.

The Weekly Cycle
1 · Prepare
Watch the week's concept video and read the assigned chapters: just the tools needed for that case.
2 · Do
Work the case analysis in Excel, plus a milestone on your own company. Check figures verify your mechanics.
3 · Discuss
Post to the role-based case board: CFO, lead lender, activist shareholder: arguing from an assigned seat.
4 · Debrief
A debrief video covers what the company actually did, the strongest arguments, and common modeling errors.

In Week 7, review two classmates' capital-structure analyses against a rubric: diagnosing someone else's model is how analysts learn.
By The End

You will be able to:

01
Diagnose a firm's cash-flow dynamics and financing needs using ratio analysis, sources-and-uses statements, and pro forma forecasting.
02
Evaluate a capital structure decision by weighing debt's tax benefits against the costs of financial distress, and recommend a target leverage level.
03
Assess payout policy and security-design decisions through the lens of market signaling and information asymmetry.
04
Build a company valuation that reconciles WACC-based DCF, Adjusted Present Value, and trading-multiple methods into a defensible value range.
05
Apply real-options reasoning to identify and value managerial flexibility in a capital-investment decision.
06
Construct a leveraged buyout model and defend a bid price and financing structure from a financial sponsor's perspective.
07
Write executive-ready decision memos and deliver a recorded investment pitch to a senior, non-technical audience.
08
Apply the semester's diagnostic and valuation toolkit to an unfamiliar company under exam conditions.

Week By Week

Three modules, 14 weeks of coursework.

Classes run August 24 – December 16, 2026, across 12 numbered weeks plus a wrap-up week and exam week. No module runs during Fall Break (week of Oct 5) or Thanksgiving Break (week of Nov 23). Click any week to see the case, readings, and what it's testing.

Module I: Cash Flow Management · Weeks 1–5
W1
Introduction & Financial Health Diagnostics
Aug 24–28 · Ratio diagnostic lab (guided Excel exercise)
▾
How cash actually moves through a firm; core diagnostic and trend ratios.
Readings: A&W Ch. 1–2; RWJ Ch. 1–3
W2
Determining Financial Health
Aug 31–Sep 4 · Butler Lumber Company
▾
Diagnose the cash demands of rapid growth; compute historical cash shortfalls; argue the loan decision from both sides (owner vs. bank lender). M0 due: select your project company.
Readings: A&W Ch. 2; RWJ Ch. 3; Higgins Ch. 2
Core concepts: Sustainable growth, trade credit, bank financing capacity.
Quantitative task: Build a historical sources-and-uses statement and a 1-year pro forma to size the financing gap.
Strategic challenge: Recommend whether the owner should accept a supplier's discount-for-cash offer and how much bank debt is safe.
W3
Pro Forma Financial Forecasting
Sep 7–11 · Nelson Nurseries (Darden)
▾
Build multi-year pro formas; calculate External Funds Required under alternative growth scenarios. M1 due: health diagnostic.
Readings: A&W Ch. 3; RWJ Ch. 3; Higgins Ch. 3–4
Core concepts: Cash flow vs. accounting profit, pro forma forecasting, External Funds Required (EFR).
Quantitative task: Construct pro forma income statements and balance sheets under alternative growth and margin assumptions.
Strategic challenge: Determine financing needs and timing as the business scales.
W4
Seasonality & Cash Flow Management
Sep 14–18 · Toy World, Inc.
▾
Model monthly cash budgets under level vs. seasonal production; size a revolving credit line to present to the bank.
Readings: A&W Ch. 4; RWJ Ch. 26–27
Core concepts: Seasonal working-capital cycles, monthly cash budgeting, line-of-credit sizing.
Quantitative task: Build a 12-month cash budget comparing level production to seasonal production.
Strategic challenge: Recommend a production strategy and the resulting peak borrowing need.
W5
Operating Strategy & Growth
Sep 21–25 · Hansson Private Label, Inc.
▾
Estimate incremental capital-budgeting cash flows tied to an operating strategy decision. M2 due: five-year pro forma.
Readings: A&W Ch. 10; RWJ Ch. 6
Core concepts: Capacity expansion, incremental cash flow analysis, capital budgeting under growth.
Quantitative task: Estimate incremental free cash flows from a new manufacturing line and compute NPV/IRR.
Strategic challenge: Advise on whether to expand capacity ahead of demand or wait for firmer signals.
Module II: Capital Structure & Financing Policy · Weeks 6–9
W6
Capital Structure & Debt Limits
Sep 28–Oct 2 · Stone Container Corporation
▾
Analyze interest tax shields, coverage, and the costs of financial distress under high leverage in a cyclical industry.
Readings: A&W Ch. 5–6; RWJ Ch. 16–17
Core concepts: Financial leverage, interest coverage, costs of financial distress, cyclical industries.
Quantitative task: Stress-test interest coverage and covenant compliance across an industry cash-flow cycle.
Strategic challenge: Assess whether the company's debt load is sustainable through a downturn.
W7
Optimal Capital Structure Workshop
Oct 12–16 · Massey Ferguson Ltd. + peer-review workshop
▾
Distress in practice: what happens when leverage meets a downturn. M3 due: capital structure analysis; calibrated peer review of two classmates.
Readings: A&W Ch. 7–8; RWJ Ch. 18
Core concepts: Costs of financial distress in practice, competing creditor interests.
Quantitative task: Assess financing needs and coverage under downturn scenarios; evaluate restructuring alternatives.
Strategic challenge: Recommend a restructuring plan that banks, governments, and shareholders could all accept.
W8
Payout Policy & Cash Distribution
Oct 19–23 · Pioneer Natural Resources
▾
Evaluate variable dividends vs. repurchases under signaling and clientele constraints; debate then vote before the debrief (CFO vs. activist investor).
Readings: A&W Ch. 11; RWJ Ch. 19
Core concepts: Dividend policy, signaling, clientele effects, share repurchases.
Quantitative task: Compare the cash-flow and EPS effects of a variable dividend program against an equivalent buyback.
Strategic challenge: Recommend a payout policy balancing signaling concerns with capital-allocation flexibility.
W9
Security Issuance & Design
Oct 26–30 · Harris Seafoods
▾
Decompose a convertible into straight-debt and option value; recommend an issuance. M4 due: payout & financing policy.
Readings: RWJ Ch. 20, 24
Core concepts: Security design, convertible debt, option value within a hybrid instrument.
Quantitative task: Decompose a convertible security into its straight-debt value and embedded conversion-option value.
Strategic challenge: Recommend convertible debt, straight debt, or equity given the risk and signaling profile.
Module III: Project & Company Valuation · Weeks 10–12
W10
Capital Budgeting & Cost of Capital
Nov 2–6 · Worldwide Paper Company
▾
Project incremental cash flows; estimate a defensible WACC; reconcile NPV/IRR conflicts. M5 due: WACC.
Readings: A&W Ch. 14–15; RWJ Ch. 5–6, 13
Core concepts: Incremental project cash flows, WACC estimation, NPV vs. IRR.
Quantitative task: Build a project-level discounted cash flow model and estimate a project-appropriate WACC.
Strategic challenge: Recommend whether to proceed and reconcile any NPV/IRR ranking conflicts.
W11
Company Valuation: WACC, APV & Multiples
Nov 9–13 · Mercury Athletic: Valuing the Opportunity
▾
Build a multi-period FCF model; unlever and relever peer betas; reconcile WACC-DCF, APV, and multiples into a bid range.
Readings: RWJ Ch. 13, 18
Core concepts: Comparable-company analysis, unlevered/relevered beta, WACC, APV, terminal value.
Quantitative task: Build a multi-year FCF forecast and value the target using WACC-DCF, APV, and trading multiples.
Strategic challenge: Recommend an acquisition price range and advise on deal structure.
W12
Real Options, M&A & LBO Capstone
Nov 16–20 · Kennecott Copper Corp.; Bidding for Hertz: sealed-bid LBO auction

Core concepts: Real options, managerial flexibility, leveraged buyouts, sponsor returns.
Quantitative task: Value an embedded real option; build an LBO model with a debt schedule and solve for sponsor IRR.
Strategic challenge: Recommend a maximum bid price consistent with the target return and assess debt capacity.

Policies

The fine print: but it matters.

Late Work

Project milestones build on one another, so timeliness matters: milestones lose 10% per day late. One case memo may be submitted up to 48 hours late without penalty, once per semester, no questions asked.

Make-Up Policy

Missed work requires advance notice whenever possible, or notice within 48 hours for a documented emergency. The Week 12 sealed-bid LBO auction and Week 7 calibrated peer review depend on the whole class submitting together: a missed one cannot simply be redone late.

Collaboration

Discussing cases with classmates is encouraged: that is how the case method works. All submitted memos, models, milestones, and exams must nonetheless be your own individual work.

AI Tools

AI assistants may be used for background research, Excel debugging, and writing polish, with disclosure in a footnote. The analysis, assumptions, and recommendation must be yours: milestone memos are graded on judgment, and the final exam and recorded pitch verify individual mastery.

Honor Code

The Virginia Tech Graduate Honor Code applies to all work in this course. For more information, refer to the GHS Constitution at the Virginia Tech Graduate Honor System.

Course Evaluations

Course evaluations are administered online at the end of the term: please take the time to complete the SPOT survey when it's sent through Canvas.
